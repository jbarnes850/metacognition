# Evidence over Source

Can fine-tuning make a model's decision to revise its answer depend on whether a critique is valid, and not on who delivers it, including for cue types and a domain it never trained on? This study fine-tunes Qwen3.5 4B to test that. The write-up is the [Training against source cues](https://jbarnes850.github.io/2026/03/20/do-models-know-when-to-change-their-mind/#training-against-source-cues) section of the main post.

## Results

Own-answer design, held-out questions. Each trial starts from the base model's first answer, so on every valid-critique trial the critique is the only route to the right answer. "Conflicting cues" pools the three held-out cue families at strengths 2 and 4. Change is conflict d-prime minus no-cue d-prime for the same arm, with a 95% interval from resampling questions.

| Arm | Training label | d-prime, no cue | d-prime, conflicting cues | Change | Valid corrections accepted | Invalid critiques accepted | Final answer equals bare-question answer | Bare-question accuracy |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `base` | none | 1.53 | 0.93 | -0.59 [-0.82, -0.42] | 84% | 29% | 62% | 79.2% |
| `main` | correct answer, cued prompts | 1.80 | 1.83 | 0.04 [-0.03, 0.10] | 15% | 3% | 96% | 83.6% |
| `nocue` | correct answer, no cues | 1.78 | 1.83 | 0.05 [-0.04, 0.14] | 20% | 3% | 95% | 83.3% |
| `bct` | base no-cue decision, crossed prompts | 1.52 | 1.53 | 0.01 [-0.09, 0.17] | 91% | 44% | 51% | 80.4% |
| `bctown` | base no-cue decision, own-answer prompts | 1.51 | 1.43 | -0.08 [-0.25, 0.06] | 86% | 37% | 54% | 80.7% |

Valid corrections accepted: no-cue hit rate on questions that arm answers wrong on its own. Invalid critiques accepted: no-cue false alarm rate. On the three red-team cue types written after the adapters were frozen, the change is -0.83 for `base`, 0.07 for `main`, 0.02 for `bct`, and -0.08 for `bctown`.

- Training on correct answers removes the cue effect by removing the critique effect. The `main` and `nocue` adapters mostly repeat their bare-question answer and accept few valid corrections.
- Consistency training (`bct`, after Chua et al., 2024) removes the cue effect and keeps critique use. It copies the base model's no-cue judgment, including its acceptance of invalid critiques, which rises from 29% to 44%.
- System 2 Attention baselines and every per-condition table are in [`evidence-over-source-results.md`](evidence-over-source-results.md), generated by `eos/report.py`.

## Design

- **Model.** Qwen/Qwen3.5-4B, bf16, thinking disabled. The decision is read from the first answer token: each option letter collects the probability of "L", "(L", and " L". This agrees with free generation on 127 of 128 checks (`eos_results/readout_check.json`).
- **Items.** 649 DS Critique Bank questions (`eos_data/items_v1.json`). A valid critique is a correct student's explanation; an invalid critique is a wrong explanation by the same student model. Both use one template. A question-blind text classifier predicts validity at AUROC 0.539, against 0.99999 on the original critiques (`eos_results/stimulus_audit.json`).
- **Splits.** Train: 235 commonsense questions. Held out: 154 commonsense questions and 260 science questions (ARC-Challenge, ARC-Easy, OpenBookQA), a domain absent from training.
- **Cue families** (`eos/cues.py`, `eos/redteam_cues.py`), each pushing toward changing or keeping the answer at strengths 1 to 4:
  - trained: reviewer panel, credential, stated confidence;
  - held out: automated verifier, role authority, messenger credibility;
  - red team, written after the adapters were frozen: emotional plea, AI consensus, claimed citation.
- **Conditions.** None (critique only), congruent (cue pushes the action the critique's validity implies), and conflict (cue pushes the opposite action).
- **Training.** LoRA rank 16, alpha 32, dropout 0.05 on attention, Gated DeltaNet, and MLP projections; AdamW, learning rate 1e-4, batch 16, one pass over 1,880 prompts (235 questions, 8 draws each), seed 0. The loss is the negative log probability of the label letter at the first answer token.
- **Baselines.** System 2 Attention (Weston and Sukhbaatar, 2023), with the published prompt and with an evidence-preserving variant, on a 150-question subset.

Deviations from the registered plan:

- The own-answer design became co-primary before any held-out trial was scored. In the crossed design, a policy that ignores every critique and gives its bare-question answer reaches d-prime 1.84, so a high crossed score cannot separate reading the critique from knowing the answer.
- `bct` and `bctown` were added after the `main` results. Their hypotheses were committed before they were trained.
- Not run: the shuffled-label arm, the instruction baseline, `bct` on the crossed core set, the crossed red-team set, and the mechanism analysis.

## Reproduce

Recompute every reported number from the committed per-trial files. CPU only, about a minute:

```bash
uv run --no-project --python 3.12 --with-requirements eos/requirements.txt eos/rerun.sh
```

Without `uv`, install `eos/requirements.txt` into a Python 3.12 environment and run `bash eos/rerun.sh`. The script checks the hashes of `eos_data/legacy_raw/`, reruns the stimulus audit, the analysis, and the report, and confirms that `eos_results/analysis.json`, `numbers.json`, `stimulus_audit.json`, and `legacy_false_flaw_qids.json` match the committed files. The pinned versions reproduce them byte for byte; other scikit-learn versions can move the audit AUROC in the third decimal.

Rerun every GPU job (hours on one GPU):

```bash
MODEL=Qwen/Qwen3.5-4B bash eos/rerun.sh --rescore
```

This rebuilds the items from DS Critique Bank, checks them against the frozen file, and runs `eos/run_gpu.sh`: base-model answers, trial files, the four adapters, every evaluation, and the S2A baselines. It needs PyTorch, Transformers, and peft; the S2A rewrites also need SGLang. The reported runs used PyTorch 2.13.0 (CUDA 13.0), Transformers 5.12.1, and peft 0.21.2 on one NVIDIA GB10, with Transformers' PyTorch implementation of the Gated DeltaNet layers (causal-conv1d was not installed). Scoring uses bf16 with left-padded batches, so a few decisions near a tie can flip with batch composition; rescoring 1,500 trials in a different order changed 13 decisions (0.9%).
